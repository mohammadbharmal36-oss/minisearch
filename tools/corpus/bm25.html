BM25 Ranking

The formula

BM25 scores a document for a query by summing, over query terms, an IDF factor multiplied by a saturated term frequency factor. The saturation is controlled by k1: after a handful of occurrences, repeating the term adds less and less score, which matches the intuition that relevance is not linear in repetition.

The parameter b controls document length normalization. At b equal to one, long documents are penalized fully relative to the average document length; at b equal to zero, length is ignored.

Why BM25 usually beats TF-IDF

Raw TF-IDF keeps rewarding repetition linearly, so a page that mentions a word a hundred times outranks a page that mentions it once, even when the repetition is spam. BM25 saturates instead. It also normalizes document length more carefully than a simple division, which keeps medium-length focused documents competitive against both thin and bloated pages.

BM25 has been the default ranking baseline of keyword search engines for decades, and it remains a strong, interpretable starting point before any learning-to-rank machinery.
